The biosecurity field, drawn as one island

Every organisation working on biosecurity, placed on a single landmass. The coastline is not decoration: it is drawn around where the organisations actually are, so a crowded part of the field becomes a wide province and a neglected one becomes a narrow spit of land.

The island reads west to east, in the order a threat is met. It makes landfall on the Prevention shore, crosses the Detection narrows, and reaches the Response interior. The southern plain is the Foundation — the funders, teachers and coordinators everything else stands on.

New to the terminology? Five phrases that unlock most of it
GCBRGlobal catastrophic biological risk — a pandemic bad enough to permanently derail civilisation, not just a severe outbreak.
Dual-use research of concernLegitimate research that could also be repurposed to cause harm. The reason some biology is published carefully.
Medical countermeasuresVaccines, drugs, diagnostics — the medical tools used against an outbreak.
Non-medical countermeasuresEverything else that blunts spread: clean indoor air, protective equipment, distancing.
Pathogen surveillanceSystematically watching for dangerous pathogens in people, animals, air or wastewater, to catch an outbreak early.

Prototype. Built on the open dataset published by biosecurity.world (entries.csv, source), which is the maintained and authoritative version of this landscape. Logos are each organisation's own favicon, shown for identification.
Reading caveats. The rings are a diagram, not geography — where a dot sits within a ring carries no meaning, only which ring it is in. Each organisation is placed once, at its primary classification in the dataset, so layer counts here run lower than those on biosecurity.world, which lists an organisation under every stage it touches. Counts reflect who is catalogued — not budget, headcount or output. A sparse ring may mean a genuine gap, a gap in the dataset's coverage, or a layer where a few very large institutions do most of the work.
