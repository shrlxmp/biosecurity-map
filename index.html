The Biosecurity Defence Chain

156 biosecurity organisations, arranged by where they act against a pandemic. Data from biosecurity.world, plus 6 organisations added here that it does not list yet.

Every layer a pandemic has to get through

Biosecurity is built as defence in depth: a series of barriers, each one catching what the last one missed. A threat has to cross three of them to reach the people at the centre: Prevention, then Detection, then Response. The further out a barrier sits, the earlier it acts.

The dashed outer ring is not a barrier. It is the foundation the three rely on: funders, educators, coordinators and reporters.

Every dot is one of the 156 organisations in the field, placed once, on the ring of its main mission.

What the map shows

    Terms used on this page
    GCBR
    Global catastrophic biological risk: a biological event bad enough to do lasting damage to civilisation, beyond a severe outbreak.
    Dual-use research of concern
    Legitimate research that could also be used to cause harm. It is why some biology is published with care.
    Medical countermeasures
    Vaccines, drugs and diagnostics, the medical tools used against an outbreak.
    Non-medical countermeasures
    Other ways to slow spread, such as clean indoor air, protective equipment and distancing.
    Pathogen surveillance
    Watching for dangerous pathogens in people, animals, air or wastewater, to catch an outbreak early.

        About the data. This map is a prototype built on the open dataset published by biosecurity.world (entries.csv, source), the maintained and authoritative version of this landscape. Newsletters and podcasts listed there are left out, because they are publications rather than organisations and most repeat an organisation already on the map. A few organisations it does not list yet are added by this map and say so in their detail panel. Each one is dropped automatically once biosecurity.world adds it. Logos are each organisation's own favicon, shown for identification.

        About the funding. Grants are Coefficient Giving's (formerly Open Philanthropy), in its two biosecurity focus areas, from the export kept by Trace, Manifund's funding database. Recipients are matched to organisations on the map by name, plus a short hand-checked list of aliases. A grant to a name the map does not use counts as not on the map.

        Reading the map. The rings are a diagram, not geography. Where a dot sits within a ring means nothing, only which ring it is in. Each organisation appears once, at its main classification in the dataset, so ring counts run lower than on biosecurity.world, which lists an organisation under every stage it touches. Counts show who is catalogued, not budget, staff or output. A sparse ring may be a real gap, a gap in the dataset, or a layer where a few large institutions do most of the work.
